## The Thread

My path started with a simple question: how can a machine move, sense, and respond in a way that feels reliable? In high school, that question came through robotics club. At NTU Mechanical Engineering, it became coursework in dynamics, machine design, control, electronics, and microprocessor systems. Outside class, it became NTU Racing, haptic research, and embedded AI projects.

The direction is now clear: I want to work on intelligent mechatronic systems where software has to respect physical constraints.

## Academic Growth

My academic record matters because it shows a change in method, not only a result. I moved from being around the top thirty in my department during freshman year toward first in the department as a junior. The public record currently includes two Dean's List certificates, with personal details redacted.

That improvement came from learning how to diagnose mistakes, adjust study strategy, and keep a stable pace across theory-heavy and project-heavy courses.

## Engineering Identity

The strongest parts of my portfolio are not isolated projects. They form a pattern:

- Haptic research trained me to think about contact, force, latency, stability, and human perception.
- NTU Racing trained me to connect embedded software with wiring, sensors, power, CAN data, and vehicle testing.
- MakeNTU and Jarvis trained me to integrate AI dialogue, microcontrollers, GUI, and appliance control into one product-like system.
- BOUTELEUR and Aero Carrier trained me to iterate through imperfect prototypes, measurements, constraints, and debugging.
